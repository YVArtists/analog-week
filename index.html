On December 1st, we welcome friends of the house Tina Guo and Derek Nakamoto for a one time concert, released on vinyl in collaboration with Elastic Stage Records

The Recording

Tuesday at 5 PM, cellist Tina Guo and pianist Derek Nakamoto will meet for the first time onstage. They have never spoken before and have not planned what they are going to play. Instead, the two musicians will spend 40 minutes discovering a piece of music together in real time, with nothing predetermined beyond the encounter itself.

The performance in front of Analog Week attendees will be recorded live and released on vinyl by Elastic Stage, available for purchase exactly as it happened, with no opportunity for edits, overdubs, or revisions afterward. It’s a chance to witness not a finished work, but the moment a musical conversation begins, and to take that moment home as a record.

Tina Guo

Cellist, composer and entrepreneur. Credits include Dune, Top Gun: Maverick and Wonder Woman. Grammy-nominated for Inner Passion in 2017.

Derek Nakamoto

Pianist, arranger and producer. Has worked with Keiko Matsui, Gerald Albright and Kazu Matsui.

About Desert Doghouse

Desert Doghouse is a six-bedroom estate near Joshua Tree, featuring antiques and art. It has three distinct living rooms, multiple patios, a large pool, pickleball, a music room, and plenty of areas to gather, or hide from the world.

The house has hosted many photo and film shoots, productions, dinners, creative projects, celebrations, and gatherings. It has been featured in the LA Times, Maxim, Vice, Robb Report and others.

Analog Week is a chance to use Desert Doghouse socially, to bring interesting people together and give guests who haven’t experienced it a reason to come see it for themselves.

Why “analog”?

We use “analog” as shorthand for things that tend to be better in person with people: cooking, talking, swimming, listening, reading, meeting someone by accident.

For four days, we’re making a little more room for those things.

What is this not?

Analog Week is not a conference, networking event, wellness retreat, or desert rager. There are no panels, forced introductions, pitching or expectation to be “on”.

Outside of the curated dinners, there is nothing you are expected to attend. Some people will arrive knowing one another, many won’t know anyone. It’s OK to sit in the shade, read a book, or break away to check out other Joshua Tree sights.

No phone baskets or digital detox rules. The idea is simply to make non-digital things more appealing: a long dinner, a swim, a record, a book, a conversation, a walk. The desert may inspire you away from your phone, but we won’t ask you to put it away.

What will happen at Analog Week?

Arrive within the window you shared with us. Drop your bag. Swim. Read. Nap. Meet someone or keep to yourself.

In the evening, we’ll gather for a curated dinner and conversation, with two live sets from Tina Guo and Derek Nakamoto.

Everything else is intentionally loose.

You do not need to stay for the night. Desert Doghouse is close enough to Los Angeles to come out in the morning, spend the day by the pool, stay for dinner, and head home that night. Alternatively, there are many other accommodations nearby.

Can I just drop in?

Yes. As long as you let us know your arrival window, you can drop in at your leisure any day from November 30 to December 3, or stop in for the day.

Is there a charge for anything?

No. There is no fee or charge for anything.

How do I best plan for this?

Don’t over-plan: this is mostly an open-house-style get-together with 10–30 other people enjoying the house with you. While the house feels secluded, major grocery stores are only 10 minutes away. The location is about two hours from L.A. Don’t think of it as a major event – think of it as a reason for a short road trip. Bring a bathing suit :)
